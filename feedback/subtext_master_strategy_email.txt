SUBTEXT: MASTER STRATEGY (CONSOLIDATED)

## 1) Core diagnosis

You did NOT build the wrong product.
The product works; the bottleneck is:

1. Positioning is still too abstract for first-time users.
2. Distribution is broad instead of persona-specific.
3. Users don’t immediately see “what do I get right now?”
4. Quick vs Deep tradeoff exists but needs clearer framing.

---

## 2) Recommended positioning (primary)

Primary value proposition:
Know what’s really in a book before assigning, recommending, or buying.

Support line:
Subtext gives teachers and parents a suitability check in under a minute, with clear content warnings and context.

Alternative headline options:
- Check what’s really in a book before it reaches your classroom.
- When shelf labels and blurbs are misleading, scan for real content.
- A suitability check in under a minute for teachers, parents, and BookTok readers.

---

## 3) Homepage structure (template-ready)

### Hero
- Headline: Know what’s really in a book in under a minute.
- Subhead: Fast suitability checks for teachers and parents.
- CTA 1: Scan a title now
- CTA 2: See example scans
- Microcopy: Quick Scan = fast triage. Deep Scan = higher-confidence analysis.

### Problem section
- Shelf/BookTok mismatch (YA labels can be misleading)
- Classroom suitability uncertainty
- Parents need context before buying

### Solution section
- Quick Scan (15–30s): first-pass triage
- Deep Scan (90–120s): richer, higher-confidence output
- Why explanations: transparent reasoning

### Trust strip
- Public beta (actively improving)
- Transparent methodology
- Indicative guidance (not official board classification)
- Privacy clarity

### How it works (3 steps)
1) Search/scan
2) Review warnings + age guidance + context
3) Decide (assign, recommend, buy, or skip)

### FAQ (must include)
- How accurate is Subtext?
- Quick vs Deep differences?
- Is this replacing human judgment?
- Is this an official rating?
- Data/privacy handling?

### Final CTA
Run your first scan
Subline: Free triage. Pay for depth.

---

## 4) Product/Monetization model

Your Quick/Deep design is correct.

Recommended packaging:
- Free tier: limited Quick scans/day
- Paid tier: Deep scans + richer outputs + exports
- Optional credit packs for heavier users

Principle:
Free triage. Pay for depth.

Ads (if used): keep out of active scan flow and warning blocks.
Best locations: results footer, bookshelf/history page, digest emails.

---

## 5) Roleplay findings (combined)

Across teacher/parent/librarian/student/book-club perspectives, the same pattern appeared:
Users want action-ready outputs after analysis.

Examples:
- Teacher: lesson starter prompts
- Parent: conversation prompts
- Librarian: batch/CSV reporting
- Student: deeper analysis mode
- Book club: compare shortlist + discussion guide

Meaning:
Your engine is good; add “last-mile decision outputs” gradually as paid/deep features.

---

## 6) 14-day execution plan

Week 1 (positioning + conversion)
1. Update homepage copy to new value prop
2. Add 3 concrete example scans above fold
3. Add explicit Quick vs Deep explanation with confidence language
4. Add FAQ trust section

Week 2 (distribution)

Success metrics for first sprint:
- Scan start rate
- Scan completion rate
- Return usage (second scan)
- Deep scan conversion intent

Do not rebuild the product.

Teacher + BookTok mismatch is a real and relatable pain.

You are close.
